\section{Reviewer 2}

\subsection{Two baselines and two noise floors for the same measurement}

The reviewer is right, and the discrepancy had two independent causes.

\textbf{The baselines.} Figure 2 and the opening of Section IV were taken from
a later re-run of the $\varepsilon = \infty$ condition alone, which overwrote
the original sweep's files --- identical filenames, identical run-order seed
--- and was never committed. Table I was computed from the original sweep. We
can defend only the committed data, so the re-run is gone and the whole paper
now comes from the original sweep: baseline \textbf{13.98 J/round} (14.0 in
Table I), mean overhead \textbf{$+51\%$}, range $+43.4\%$ to $+62.2\%$,
board-power floor \textbf{7.80\%}. The 14.5\,J / $+45\%$ / 8.2\% / 9.46\% set
is withdrawn.

\textbf{The floor comparison was the wrong instrument}, and this is the more
important half of the reviewer's point. Two things were wrong with it:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2em
\item The 12.5\% spread is a spread in \emph{net energy per round}, while the
7.80\% floor is a spread in \emph{board power}. The two differ by the round
duration, which also varies across seeds. Every floor in the paper is now
measured on the quantity it bounds. On energy per round the floor is
$\pm 2.58$\,J/round, \textbf{12.0\%} --- not 7.8\% --- so the 12.5\% spread
sits essentially on it.
\item Even with matched units, a floor is the spread of \emph{one} condition's
seeds, whereas the claim is about the spread of \emph{five condition means},
which with three seeds each is narrower by about $\sqrt{3}$. So we test the
claim instead of comparing it to a floor: permuting the seed labels across the
five private budgets gives \textbf{$F = 1.46$, $p = 0.27$} on HAR and
\textbf{$F = 0.46$, $p = 0.79$} on CIFAR-10.
\end{itemize}

On the specific contrast the reviewer raises, $+43.4\%$ at $\varepsilon = 1$
against $+62.2\%$ at $\varepsilon = 4$: it is not resolvable. The three seeds
of the $\varepsilon = 4$ condition span 20.9 to 24.1\,J/round on their own, and
the overheads are not ordered in $\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon = 8$ sits below
$\varepsilon = 4$).

Section IV-A is retitled \emph{The Per-Round Cost Does Not Track the Budget}
and states the weaker claim the data supports: no resolvable dependence on
$\varepsilon$, and explicitly \textbf{no claim of equality} --- three seeds per
budget bound a budget effect at about the size of the overhead's own scatter,
not at zero.

\subsection{The guarantee is mislabelled, and $q_c$ assumes what we disclaim}

Accepted in full, on both halves.

\textbf{The label.} Opacus clips and noises per-sample gradients inside local
training, so the guarantee is \textbf{example-level}, applied locally, with the
budget held per client over the whole run. It is not the client-level
(user-level) DP of [2] and [11], which noises the entire client update and
conceals participation itself. We have relabelled the abstract, Section I,
Section II-D, Section III-B, Section V-E and the conclusion, and Section III-B
now states the relation explicitly: our guarantee is weaker, and the per-round
overhead we measure is the per-sample gradient work that both mechanisms need,
so the energy result is orthogonal to the choice between them.

\textbf{The $q_c$ factor.} Also accepted: $q_c$ makes $s_{\text{total}}$ an
expected participation count, nothing in the protocol bounds the realised one,
and the reported $\varepsilon$ is therefore \textbf{optimistic rather than an
upper bound}. We had begun auditing this before the reviews and the audit
agrees with the reviewer in every run. Recomputing each client's spent
$\varepsilon$ from the participation logs, the worst client overspends in
\textbf{all 29 private runs}, by 4\% to 22\%:

\medskip
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
target $\varepsilon$ & 0.5 & 1 & 2 & 4 & 8 \\
\midrule
worst spent, UCI-HAR  & 0.570 & 1.122 & 2.285 & 4.250 & 9.112 \\
worst spent, CIFAR-10 & 0.611 & 1.162 & 2.251 & 4.356 & 8.850 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\medskip

Table I now carries a \emph{Worst $\varepsilon$} column with the HAR values,
and Section III-B states the remedy the reviewer names: a guarantee that must
hold unconditionally has to drop $q_c$ and calibrate against
$R \, E \, \lceil n/B \rceil$, or cap participation per client. The audit script
has a \texttt{-{}-worst-case} mode that prints the $\varepsilon$ of
participation in every round, which is the figure such a calibration would have
to use.

We would rather report this than hide it: the same optimistic calibration is
common in federated DP simulations, and the audit is a few lines of logging
plus a replay.

\subsection{Two results carry less information than their framing}

Accepted on both.

\textbf{The post-peak energy share.} The reviewer is right that with per-round
energy flat, ``83\% of the joules'' is 83 of 100 rounds restated, and right
that the declines defining those peaks sat below our own floors. A peak is now
reported only where the peak-to-final drop clears that condition's across-seed
spread in final accuracy \emph{and} the individual seeds agree on where the
peak falls. Under that test:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.2em
\item \textbf{HAR fails at every budget.} The largest drop in a mean curve is
0.032 at $\varepsilon = 1$ against a $\pm 0.042$ spread, and the per-seed peak
rounds disagree (10, 33, 39 at $\varepsilon = 1$; 16, 54, 65 at
$\varepsilon = 0.5$). The HAR stopping-round claim is withdrawn and Figure 1 is
redrawn with no marker, its caption saying why.
\item \textbf{CIFAR-10 passes at the two tightest budgets.}
$\varepsilon = 0.5$: drop 0.026 against $\pm 0.010$, seeds peaking at rounds 8,
8 and 9. $\varepsilon = 1$: drop 0.044 against $\pm 0.014$, seeds between 10
and 16. At $\varepsilon \ge 2$ the drops fall inside their spreads and we claim
nothing.
\end{itemize}

Section IV-C now leads with the peak's position --- round 8 of 30 at
$\varepsilon = 0.5$ --- and says in the text that the energy fraction tracks
the fraction of the schedule after the peak precisely because per-round energy
is flat, so the joules price the tail of a round budget the designer chose. The
monotone peak ordering 7/11/19/24 is withdrawn: only two budgets resolve a peak
at all.

\textbf{The carbon factor.} Also accepted. The ratio is the two grid
intensities and would be identical for non-private training, and the paper now
says so in Section IV-F. The abstract and Section IV-F lead instead with the
quantity that is about privacy: the premium attributable to privacy alone,
106--148\,gCO$_2$eq per 1000 runs on Algeria's grid against 5.7--7.9\,g in
Norway. The geographic point is framed as context for a federated deployment,
which cannot relocate the way a data centre can.
