\section{Conclusion}

We measured what differential privacy costs in energy when added to federated
learning, using hardware counters around each round on two datasets, with
UCI-HAR partitioned so each client holds one study participant's data.

The per-round cost is power times time. The power factor is $1.19\times$ on
both datasets to within half a per cent, despite different models and a
fourfold difference in size --- the arithmetic of per-sample gradients and
clipping, apparently a hardware constant. The time factor is not transferable,
and their product, $+51\%$ and $+34\%$, carries no systematic budget
dependence. Everything that does acts through the round count, and it is the
larger term: reaching $0.70$ accuracy on HAR costs $5.7\times$ the non-private
energy with only $\varepsilon \ge 4$ arriving at all, while $0.85$ is
unreachable at any expenditure.

Three rules follow. Derive the round budget from the privacy budget rather
than the task, since rounds consume budget and training to convergence is
self-defeating. Choose the privacy budget and the accuracy requirement
together, because a budget implies a ceiling no computation reaches. And check
whether the private model is noise-limited before tuning it: the same two
knobs that move nothing on HAR move accuracy by a factor of two on CIFAR-10.
Paid in joules, the cost also inherits the local carbon intensity --- an
$18.8\times$ range between Algeria's grid and Norway's for an identical
guarantee, on both datasets --- which a privacy--utility evaluation cannot
represent.

We also report two hazards. Instrumentation attached to a privacy mechanism
scales with the privacy parameter, so its cost mimics the effect under study.
And calibrating the noise against expected client participation under-charges
the clients sampled most: all 29 of our private runs spend more than their
stated budget, by up to $22\%$. Deriving noise multipliers before measurement,
verifying step counts per round, randomising run order and recomputing each
client's spent budget from logged participation are cheap controls for any
such study.

Clients are virtual and share one GPU, so the ratios transfer but the absolute
joules do not, and our two datasets bound the effects rather than establishing
them: a stopping round is resolvable on CIFAR-10 below $\varepsilon = 2$ and
on UCI-HAR nowhere. What sets that boundary --- task difficulty, the magnitude
of the noise, or their ratio --- is the question this work leaves open.
