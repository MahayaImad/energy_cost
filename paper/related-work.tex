% Section II -- Related Work.
% Positioning: prior work either optimises energy under a DP constraint
% analytically, or measures one privacy setting. Neither sweeps epsilon with
% hardware counters, and none reports energy-to-target-accuracy.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Measuring the energy of training}

Green AI made energy and carbon reportable quantities rather than
externalities~\cite{greenai, strubell}, and tools now estimate them from
utilisation traces and grid factors~\cite{henderson}. Comparisons against
instrumented hardware find those estimates diverge substantially from
measurement~\cite{bouza}, which is why we read NVML counters instead. Patterson et al.~\cite{patterson} established
that where a job runs changes its emissions by $5$--$10\times$; our geographic
result applies that to a parameter rather than to a schedule. Savazzi et
al.~\cite{savazzi} give an analytical energy and carbon framework for
federated learning, but without a privacy mechanism.

\subsection{Energy and differential privacy in federated learning}

One line of work treats DP noise as a constraint inside a wireless
resource-allocation problem and minimises device energy under it, either by
harvesting channel noise as the privacy mechanism~\cite{liu, zhang} or by
co-designing compression with the noise budget~\cite{chen}. Energy there
is an optimisation objective evaluated through a convergence bound; it is not
measured, and $\varepsilon$ is a constraint rather than a swept variable.
Closest to us empirically, Schwermer et al.~\cite{schwermer} built a
physically distributed edge testbed and measured traffic, energy and training
time for a CNN on MNIST with DP enabled, finding software estimates deviating
by up to $35\%$. Their contrast is DP on versus off at one setting; ours is
the shape of the cost across the budget. Aroussi et al.~\cite{aroussi}
schedule $\varepsilon$ adaptively on Raspberry Pi and ESP32 nodes for a
$20$--$25\%$ energy gain --- the gain of a controller, not the cost curve a
designer needs to choose the budget.

\subsection{Rounds as the mechanism}

That the cost should act through the round count is known analytically. Wei
et al.~\cite{nbafl} prove that under client-level DP there is an optimal
number of aggregations for a given protection level, beyond which convergence
degrades, and later derive a method to find it~\cite{weicdp}; the same optimum
reappears for personalised federated learning and in control formulations
adapting noise and rounds jointly~\cite{yuan}. These results say where to stop but not what continuing
costs, the round being an abstract unit in them. We price it, and find the
optimum resolvable only where the budget is tight enough for the decline to
clear the measurement noise.

\subsection{Our position}

We therefore contribute the missing empirical axis: hardware-counter energy
swept across $\varepsilon$ under whole-run client-level
accounting~\cite{dpsgd, geyer}, decomposed into a per-round overhead that
carries no systematic budget dependence and a convergence penalty that
carries all of it, and reported as energy-to-target-accuracy rather than
energy per round or energy per run. Two of our findings are about measuring
this at all rather than about the result, and neither appears in the work
above: an instrument attached to a privacy mechanism scales with the privacy
parameter, and calibrating client-level noise against expected participation
under-charges the clients that happen to be sampled most.
