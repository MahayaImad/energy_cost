\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Federated Setup}

Both datasets run on Flower~\cite{flower} with FedAvg, $50\%$ of clients
sampled per round for training and $50\%$ for evaluation, one local epoch
(batch 32, SGD at lr $0.01$, momentum $0.9$), and three seeds per
configuration.

\textbf{UCI-HAR}~\cite{uci-har} is primary. We keep the published
subject-disjoint split: its 21 training participants become the federation's
clients, one per person, and the 9 test participants are held out entirely, so
central accuracy measures generalisation to people the federation never saw.
\emph{Clients correspond to individual study participants}, so the partition
is given by who contributed the data rather than by a heterogeneity parameter.
Each client holds about 279 training windows. We train on the raw inertial
signals (nine channels, 128 samples per $2.56$~s window) with a three-block 1D
CNN and global average pooling ($37$k parameters), close enough to the
CIFAR-10 model's $62$k that per-round energy stays comparable. Neither model
uses batch normalisation, which Opacus~\cite{opacus} rejects.

Local train and validation sets are split \emph{within each activity class},
in file order, discarding the straddling window. HAR windows overlap by
$50\%$, so a random split would place two halves of one window on opposite
sides of the line; and participants record the six activities in blocks, so a
single ordered cut would leave clients training on a subset of the classes.
Splitting within each class satisfies both constraints.

We train HAR for 100 rounds: the non-private model reaches $95\%$ of its final
accuracy only at round 59, so a shorter budget could not distinguish
convergence from truncation. The budget is not a free parameter here, since it
enters the accounting directly.

\textbf{CIFAR-10} is secondary: a five-layer CNN ($62$k parameters) across 20
clients over 30 rounds, partitioned by a Dirichlet distribution over labels at
$\alpha = 0.5$~\cite{dirichlet}.

\subsection{Privacy Accounting}

We enforce \emph{client-level local differential privacy}: each client holds
its own $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ budget over the entire run, and the server is
untrusted. The budget is accounted over all local SGD steps a client takes
across all rounds in which it participates --- not per round, and not per
local epoch. For a client with $n$ examples, batch $B$, $R$ rounds, sampling
fraction $q_c$ and $E$ local epochs:
%
\begin{equation}
  s_{\text{total}} = R \, q_c \, E \, \lceil n / B \rceil
\end{equation}
%
We invert the RDP accountant~\cite{rdp} once, before training, to find the
noise multiplier $\sigma$ giving the target $\varepsilon$ at
$\delta = 10^{-5}$ after $s_{\text{total}}$ steps, and hold it constant.
Precomputation is deliberate: in simulation each client is reconstructed every
round, so an engine-internal accountant resets each round and reports a
per-round $\varepsilon$ while the true budget compounds.

Two properties deserve statement. Opacus samples at rate
$1/\lceil n/B \rceil$ rather than $B/n$, and we calibrate at the rate the
sampler actually uses. And we claim \textbf{no amplification by client
subsampling}, which would require secure aggregation or a shuffler; the
reported $\varepsilon$ is an upper bound.

Because per-client dataset sizes differ, $\sigma$ differs per client
(Table~\ref{tab:perround}). The two datasets sit in different regimes: a HAR
client holds $279$ windows against CIFAR-10's $2000$, so the sampling rate is
roughly seven times higher, and with the longer budget HAR's convergence
requires, $\sigma$ at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ reaches $17.5$--$20.6$ against
$2.8$--$6.1$. Under local DP the round budget is therefore not free:
extending training to reach convergence raises $\sigma$ for every round.

\subsection{Energy Measurement}

Energy comes from the NVML counter
\texttt{nvmlDeviceGetTotalEnergyConsumption}~\cite{nvml}, which accumulates
millijoules on the device. Differencing it before and after each round
involves no power sampling and no integration, and cannot miss a transient.
This is measured, not estimated from a TDP model.

Measurement is server-side and per round, bracketing \texttt{configure\_train}
through \texttt{aggregate\_evaluate}. All clients share one GPU in simulation,
so a round-level window captures the full cost regardless of actor scheduling;
measuring inside each client would double-count concurrent work.

We report energy gross and net of an idle baseline, sampled per run after
waiting for board power to stabilise: a card does not reach its floor when a
run ends, and probing during that tail reads a busy baseline that understates
the run's net energy. Baselines are also checked against the sweep median
($18.21$~W on HAR) and outliers discarded. Net energy subtracts the baseline
over the wall-clock the counter covers, which excludes start-up, central
evaluation and teardown.

Three seeds per configuration give a measurement noise floor of $\pm 5.43$~W
($9.46\%$) on HAR and $\pm 4.38$~W ($6.38\%$) on CIFAR-10. No smaller effect
is reported as an effect. The HAR floor is wider because its rounds last about
$0.48$~s, too short to average out power fluctuation; whole-run totals are the
more reliable quantity there.

\subsection{Experimental Controls}

\textbf{Clipping norm $C = 1.0$ for every $\varepsilon$}, since tuning it per
budget would confound the comparison.

\textbf{Identical compute across budgets.} We verify per round that executed
optimiser steps match those the accountant was calibrated for: on HAR all six
conditions execute 89 steps per round, and the counts agree exactly.

\textbf{The accountant is excluded from the measurement window.} Its search
costs $0.086$~s at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ against $0.693$~s at $\varepsilon = 8$,
so run inside the loop it scales with $\varepsilon$ inside the measured
window. In our first clean sweep it reproduced the entire observed trend
(predicted $+18.2$~s at $\varepsilon = 8$ over $\varepsilon = 0.5$; measured
$+19.7$~s) while step counts stayed flat. All $\sigma$ values are derived
before measurement begins.

\textbf{Randomised run order.} Sweeping $\varepsilon$ in a fixed order leaves
execution order and $\varepsilon$ correlated at $\rho = -0.99$, so drift over
a sweep is inseparable from a privacy effect. Randomising reduces this to
$\rho = -0.20$.

\textbf{Determinism deliberately not enforced.} Enabling deterministic kernels
would change measured energy, i.e.\ measure a configuration nobody deploys.
Reproducibility comes from averaging over seeds.

\subsection{Limitations}

Clients are virtual and share one GPU, though on HAR each holds one real
participant's data: relative comparisons hold, absolute per-device joules do
not transfer. CPU RAPL counters were unavailable, so measurement is GPU-only
and communication energy is modelled. The workload does not saturate the
device ($60$--$65$~W on a $360$~W card), which is why net figures accompany
gross ones.

Two HAR choices are worth stating. Per-channel standardisation constants are
computed from the training split and are global across clients, so a federated
deployment would have to agree them without pooling raw data. And the round
budget is set from the non-private convergence curve, so private conditions
inherit a longer budget than any of them needs and a correspondingly larger
$\sigma$; their accuracies are a lower bound on what a per-$\varepsilon$ tuned
budget would give.
