\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Two Costs, Two Remedies}

The per-round overhead is an implementation cost, and the decomposition says
which part. The power factor is the same on both datasets, so it is the
arithmetic of per-sample gradients and clipping and will yield only to better
kernels or amortised clipping; the time factor is not, so it is where a
scheduling or batching change could act. Neither varies systematically with
the budget, and everything that does acts through the round count, bounded by
the privacy accounting rather than by any implementation. An evaluation
reporting energy per round sees only the first and concludes that privacy
costs a third to a half again; measured against the accuracy a deployment
needs, the second is several times larger and sometimes infinite. The third
axis therefore does not merely rank configurations, it changes which
engineering effort is worth spending.

\subsection{Privacy Sets a Quality Ceiling}

The cost of privacy is not a constant multiplier: on HAR it is invisible at
$0.40$ accuracy, $5.7\times$ at $0.70$, and unbounded at $0.85$. Past the
ceiling a budget implies, the trade is not energy for accuracy but nothing for
nothing. The usual formulation --- choose $\varepsilon$, then see what utility
follows --- treats the two as sequential; our results suggest choosing them
jointly, since a utility-only evaluation reports a lower number without saying
whether more computation would fix it. It would not: the round budget is part
of the privacy budget, so training to convergence is self-defeating, the
extension being what prevents convergence.

\subsection{When Tuning Works Is Itself a Finding}

The same two-knob experiment is inert on HAR and decisive on CIFAR-10, and
that contrast is more useful than either result alone: whether hyperparameter
search is worth its energy under DP depends on whether the private model still
retains signal to lose. Where it does not, the levers that remain are the
privacy and round budgets. And increasing local work per round --- the
standard way to buy fewer rounds --- stops paying on HAR and actively harms on
CIFAR-10, because local steps enter $s_{\text{total}}$ and force a larger
$\sigma$.

\subsection{Where the Computation Runs}

Measured in joules, the cost inherits the carbon intensity of wherever they
are drawn: an $18.8\times$ range between Algeria's grid and Norway's for
identical computation and an identical guarantee. The premium came out the
same on both datasets despite a fivefold difference in energy per round, so
the ratio belongs to the privacy mechanism rather than the workload. The point
is sharper for federated learning than for centralised training, because the
computation sits on whatever grids the participating devices do and no
operator chooses them.

\subsection{Two Ways to Measure a Privacy Mechanism Wrongly}

Instrumentation attached to a privacy mechanism tends to scale with the
privacy parameter, so its cost mimics the effect under study: the accountant's
calibration search, run inside the federated loop, reproduced our entire
observed $\varepsilon$ trend while step counts stayed flat, and nothing in the
resulting numbers announces it. The second hazard is an accounting error
rather than a measurement one, and it survived into our own first draft.
Both are generic to client-level DP in simulation, so we recommend four
controls: derive noise multipliers before measurement begins, verify per round
that executed and calibrated step counts agree, randomise run order, and
recompute each client's spent budget from logged participation rather than
reporting the target.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}

Clients are virtual and share one GPU, so the ratios transfer to real devices
but the absolute joules do not; confirming them on physical hardware is the
immediate next step. Measurement is GPU-only and communication energy is
modelled --- for a $0.15$--$0.24$~MB model over tens of rounds we expect it
small, but have not shown it. One CIFAR-10 run is excluded for being measured
in a different session, leaving that condition on two seeds. And our two
datasets bound the effects rather than establishing them: the stopping round
appears on one and not the other, and the tuning result inverts between them,
so where the boundary falls is a question this work poses rather than
settles.
