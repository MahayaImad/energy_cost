\section{Results}

All figures are means over three seeds, against noise floors of $9.46\%$ on
HAR and $6.38\%$ on CIFAR-10; no smaller difference is treated as resolvable.

\subsection{The Per-Round Cost Is Fixed}

Net of idle, private training on HAR costs $21.0$~J per round against
$14.5$~J without privacy, an overhead of $+45\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:perround}). Across the five private conditions the figure
varies by $8.2\%$ of its mean, inside the noise floor, so the per-round cost
does not depend on $\varepsilon$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:perround}). CIFAR-10 agrees at $+60\%$ with a $9.6\%$
spread. Wall-clock agrees: every private condition takes $47.8$--$48.3$~s for 100
rounds against $37.2$~s without privacy, a $1.2\%$ spread against an $11$~s
step, with all six executing 89 optimiser steps per round.

The two mechanisms are therefore separable. The per-round overhead is an
implementation cost --- per-sample gradients and clipping --- paid identically
at any budget. Everything $\varepsilon$-dependent acts through the number of
rounds needed. Reporting only energy per round would show privacy costing a
flat $45\%$ and miss the larger effect.

\subsection{The Cost Grows with the Target}

Energy per round makes privacy look like a flat surcharge. Measured against
what a deployment wants --- a model of a given quality --- it is not flat at
all (Table~\ref{tab:target}). At $0.40$ accuracy every condition costs
$178$--$218$~J net, all within the noise floor: privacy is free at this
quality. At $0.50$ the private conditions cost $1.17$--$2.10\times$ the
non-private $204$~J. At $0.70$ the survivors cost $\mathbf{5.8\times}$
($1753$~J at $\varepsilon = 4$ against $300$~J), and $\varepsilon \le 2$ never
arrive. At $0.85$, which the non-private model reaches in $57.7$ rounds and
$878$~J, no budget we tested arrives at any expenditure.

Unreachability is the sharpest form of the cost: above a budget-dependent
ceiling a deployment cannot buy the accuracy at all, because the training that
would close the gap is what raises $\sigma$.

\subsection{An Energy-Optimal Stopping Round}

Under a sufficiently tight budget the accuracy curve peaks and declines
(Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}), so training past the peak spends energy and
loses accuracy at once. On HAR this
appears at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ and only there: the model peaks at $0.4712$ at
round 17 and ends at $0.4553$, the following 83 rounds accounting for
$2477$~J, or $83\%$ of the run. At $\varepsilon \ge 1$ the HAR curves are
still flat or rising at round 100.

CIFAR-10, over 30 rounds, shows the effect across more budgets, the peak round
ordering monotonically: 7 at $\varepsilon = 0.5$, then 11, 19, 24, and no peak
before round 30 at $\varepsilon \ge 8$. At $\varepsilon = 0.5$ the model
reaches $0.169$ at round 7 and ends at $0.161$, the remaining rounds
accounting for $77\%$ of the run.

The datasets bound the effect rather than replicating it: the mechanism is the
same --- accumulated noise eventually dominates the aggregated update --- but
where the crossover falls depends on the task. We state the finding
conditionally. A tight budget induces an energy-optimal stopping round well
before the budget expires, and training for a fixed round count regardless of
$\varepsilon$ risks burning energy while degrading the model; we do not claim
the monotone ordering generalises, since HAR does not reproduce it.

\subsection{CIFAR-10 as a Secondary Check}

CIFAR-10 reproduces the per-round result ($+60\%$ net of idle, flat to within
$9.6\%$) and gives the stopping effect across more budgets. Reaching $0.20$
accuracy costs $1482$~J net at $\varepsilon = 1$ against $406$~J without
privacy, $3.65\times$, because the private run needs $11.7$ rounds against
$2.3$; at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ it is never reached. Absolute accuracies are low
($0.503$ without privacy, $0.16$--$0.31$ under it), as expected for a $62$k
CNN over 30 non-IID rounds, which is why HAR carries the headline claims.

\subsection{Ablations}

We vary one factor at a time at $\varepsilon \in \{1, \infty\}$, three seeds
per cell. On HAR only local epochs and the clipping norm are meaningful, since
the federation size is fixed by the split and there is no Dirichlet parameter.

\textbf{Under a tight budget the model becomes noise-limited.} At
$\varepsilon = 1$ on HAR neither factor moves accuracy: final accuracy spans
$2.6\%$ of its mean across one, two and five local epochs and $2.3\%$ across
$C \in \{0.5, 1, 2\}$, both inside the $9.46\%$ floor. With $\sigma$ near
$10$, the injected noise dominates what these settings contribute, and tuning
them is not a route to a better private model.

\textbf{The standard efficiency lever stops paying.} More local work per round
is how federated learning ordinarily buys fewer rounds, and on HAR without
privacy it works: energy to reach $0.70$ falls from $304$~J at one local epoch
to $192$~J at five, a $1.6\times$ saving at unchanged accuracy. Under
$\varepsilon = 1$ the saving vanishes --- the target is reached at no epoch
count --- while energy per round still rises from $29$~J to $64$~J. Local
steps enter $s_{\text{total}}$, so extra local work forces a larger $\sigma$
and cancels the convergence gain.

\textbf{On a harder task the lever inverts.} On CIFAR-10 at
$\varepsilon = 1$, accuracy falls from $0.178$ to $0.134$ to $0.101$ across
the same axis ($56\%$ against a $6.38\%$ floor), the peak round collapses from
13 to 4, and post-peak waste grows from $2596$~J to $14819$~J.

\textbf{Clipping is free in compute.} $C$ leaves energy per round unchanged on
both datasets ($29$~J across all three values on HAR), so it acts purely on
convergence --- not at all on HAR, but strongly on CIFAR-10 in a way final
accuracy hides: $C = 2$ peaks at $0.191$ at round 8 and ends at $0.102$,
giving back $46.5\%$ of its peak against $3.1\%$ at $C = 0.5$. Our fixed
$C = 1.0$ is conservative rather than optimal.

\subsection{Carbon Cost by Grid}

Table~\ref{tab:carbon} and Fig.~\ref{fig:carbon} scale the measured HAR
energy to 1000 runs and apply published grid carbon intensities; the scaling repeats the measured workload
rather than extrapolating to other hardware. The cost attributable to privacy
ranges from $105$--$133$~gCO$_2$eq on Algeria's gas-dominated grid to
$5.6$--$7.1$~g in Norway, a factor of $18.8$ for the same computation and the
same guarantee. Selecting a privacy budget is therefore partly a geographic
decision, a consideration absent from the privacy--utility framing.
