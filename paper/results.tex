\section{Results}
\label{sec:Results}

All figures are over three seeds, against noise floors taken as the worst
within-condition across-seed spread: in power, $7.8\%$ on HAR and $8.9\%$ on
CIFAR-10; in final accuracy, $\pm 0.051$ and $\pm 0.031$. No smaller
difference is resolvable.

\subsection{The Per-Round Cost Is Fixed}

On HAR, private training consumes $21.1$~J per round against $14.0$~J without privacy, an overhead of $+51\%$ 
(Table~\ref{tab:perround}, Fig.~\ref{fig:perround}). The overhead combines $+26.7\%$ wall-clock and
$+19.0\%$ mean power, so a power ratio alone would report $19\%$. Across the five private budgets the spread is $12.5\%$ of the mean against a $7.8\%$ noise floor, and it does not order with $\varepsilon$: we claim no
systematic dependence rather than equality. CIFAR-10 shows the same pattern ($+34\%$, spread $6.0\%$, inside its $8.9\%$ floor).

These results separate the two sources of energy cost: the per-round overhead comes from the DP operations and does not order with $\varepsilon$, while everything budget-dependent acts through the number of rounds a target accuracy needs, which per-round energy cannot see.

\subsection{The Cost Grows with the Target}

Energy per round suggests that privacy adds a fixed cost, but the total cost rises sharply with the target accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:target}). Every entry in Table~\ref{tab:target} is a mean over all three seeds except the one marked $\ddagger$, which two of three reached. At $0.40$ accuracy, privacy adds no measurable cost for $\varepsilon \geq 1$ ($187$--$210$ J against $185$ J without it). The private runs reach this first target in fewer rounds than the non-private one, which we attribute to noise acting as regularization early in training; the effect reverses at every higher target.

At $0.50$, private training requires $1.28$--$1.79\times$ the non-private energy, rising to $5.7\times$ at $0.70$; $\varepsilon \le 2$ does not reach this target. At $0.85$, the non-private model reaches the target in $57.0$ rounds and $839$~J, while no tested private budget reaches it.

\subsection{An Energy-Optimal Stopping Round}

Under a sufficiently tight budget the accuracy curve peaks and declines, so
training past the peak spends energy and loses accuracy at once. We report a
stopping round only where the peak-to-final drop exceeds that condition's
across-seed spread and the seeds agree on where the peak falls.

On CIFAR-10 it resolves at the two tightest budgets. At $\varepsilon = 0.5$
the mean peaks at $0.176$ at round 8 and ends at $0.150$, a drop of $0.026$
against $\pm 0.010$, the seeds peaking at rounds 8, 8 and 9; the rounds after
the peak account for $60$--$68\%$ of the run's net energy. At $\varepsilon =
1$ the drop is $0.044$ against $\pm 0.014$, with $42$--$61\%$ of the energy
spent after a peak the seeds place between rounds 10 and 16. At $\varepsilon
\ge 2$ the drops fall inside their spreads.

On HAR (Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}) no budget resolves one over 100 rounds.
Seeds do peak early and give accuracy back---at $\varepsilon = 1$ one peaks at
round 10 and loses $0.079$, spending $85\%$ of its energy afterwards---but the
peak rounds disagree (10, 33 and 39 at $\varepsilon = 1$; 16, 54 and 65 at
$\varepsilon = 0.5$) and the drop in the mean stays inside the $\pm 0.042$
spread of final accuracy. The mechanism is visible per run; its location does
not survive averaging.

The mechanism is the same on both---accumulated noise eventually dominates the
aggregated update---but where the crossover falls depends on the task and, on
HAR, on the seed. So we state it conditionally: a tight enough budget induces
an energy-optimal stopping round well before the budget expires, and training
for a fixed round count regardless of $\varepsilon$ risks burning energy while
degrading the model. We claim no ordering of the peak round in $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{CIFAR-10 as a Secondary Check}

CIFAR-10 reproduces the per-round result ($+34\%$ net of idle, spread $6.0\%$
inside an $8.9\%$ floor) and carries the stopping effect. Reaching $0.20$
accuracy costs $1076$~J net at $\varepsilon = 1$ against $677$~J without
privacy, $1.59\times$, because the private run needs $8.0$ rounds against
$3.7$; at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ only two of three seeds reach it. Absolute
accuracies are low ($0.490$ without privacy, $0.15$--$0.30$ under it), as
expected for a $62$k CNN over 30 non-IID rounds, which is why HAR carries the
headline claims.

\subsection{Ablations}

We vary one factor at a time at $\varepsilon \in \{1,\infty\}$ with three seeds per cell. On HAR only local epochs and the clipping norm vary: the federation size is fixed by the data split and there is no Dirichlet parameter.

\textbf{Under a tight budget, the model becomes noise-limited.} At $\varepsilon=1$ on HAR, neither local epochs nor clipping norm moves final accuracy outside the $\pm 0.041$ across-seed floor of these cells. With $\sigma\approx10$, injected noise dominating these settings, tuning them does not improve the private model.

\textbf{The usual efficiency lever stops paying.} Without privacy, increasing local epochs from one to five reduces the energy needed to reach $0.70$ accuracy from $345$~J to $193$~J, a $1.79\times$ saving. At $\varepsilon=1$, however, the target is not reached at any epoch count, while per-round energy rises from $22$~J to $49$~J. More local steps increase $s_{\text{total}}$, which raises $\sigma$ and offsets the potential convergence benefit.

\textbf{On CIFAR-10, the effect reverses.} At $\varepsilon=1$, accuracy drops from $0.182$ to $0.114$ to $0.100$ as local work increases, well beyond the $\pm 0.024$ accuracy floor of these cells. The peak round falls from 15 to 6, while post-peak energy waste rises from $1785$~J to $7312$~J.

\textbf{Clipping affects convergence, not computation.} Changing $C$ leaves per-round energy essentially unchanged on both datasets, but it can affect convergence, particularly on CIFAR-10. For example, at $C=2$ it peaks at $0.187$ but falls to $0.100$ by the end, losing $46.7\%$ of its peak accuracy. Thus, the fixed $C=1.0$ used in our main experiments is conservative rather than optimal.

\subsection{Carbon Cost by Grid}

Table~\ref{tab:carbon} and Fig.~\ref{fig:carbon} scale the measured HAR
energy to 1000 runs and apply published grid carbon
intensities~\cite{energy2024yearly}; the scaling repeats the measured
workload rather than extrapolating to other hardware. The cost attributable to privacy
ranges from $106$--$148$~gCO$_2$eq on Algeria's gas-dominated grid to
$5.7$--$7.9$~g in Norway, a factor of $18.8$ for the same computation and the
same guarantee.
