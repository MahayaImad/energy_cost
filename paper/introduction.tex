\section{Introduction}

Differential privacy in federated learning is evaluated almost exclusively
along two axes: a budget $\varepsilon$ is chosen, the resulting accuracy is
reported, and the trade-off between them is the result~\cite{dpsgd, opacus}.

A third axis is missing. Adding differential privacy changes how much energy
training consumes, and on the battery-powered edge devices federated learning
exists to serve, energy decides whether a deployment is possible at all. A
privacy configuration a phone cannot afford to run is not a point on the
privacy--utility curve; it is off the curve. Green AI research has established
that energy and carbon deserve first-class measurement~\cite{greenai,
strubell}, but has concentrated on large centralised training, while federated
work on the energy of differential privacy has mostly minimised it
analytically under a fixed guarantee~\cite{chen, zhang} or measured it at a
single privacy setting~\cite{schwermer}. The shape of the cost as
$\varepsilon$ varies --- what a designer needs in order to choose a budget ---
is what we measure here, with NVML hardware counters differenced around each
federated round rather than estimated from device power ratings.

\textbf{The per-round cost is power times time, and only one factor
transfers.} Private rounds draw $1.19\times$ the power of non-private ones on
both datasets, to within $0.5\%$, despite different models and a fourfold
difference in size: the arithmetic of per-sample gradients and clipping, and
apparently a hardware constant. The duration factor is $1.27\times$ on UCI-HAR
and $1.13\times$ on CIFAR-10. Their product, $+51\%$ and $+34\%$, carries no
systematic dependence on $\varepsilon$, so everything budget-dependent acts
through the number of rounds --- which an energy-per-round evaluation cannot
see.

\textbf{The cost grows with the accuracy demanded, until it is unbounded.} On
HAR, private and non-private training cost the same energy to reach $0.40$
accuracy, $1.28$--$1.79\times$ to reach $0.50$, and $5.7\times$ to reach
$0.70$ --- where only $\varepsilon \ge 4$ arrives. At $0.85$, which the
non-private model reaches in 57 rounds, no budget we tested arrives at any
expenditure: under client-level accounting the extra training that would close
the gap is itself what raises the noise.

\textbf{The same budget carries very different carbon costs.} The privacy
premium on 1000 HAR runs is $106$--$148$~gCO$_2$eq on Algeria's gas-dominated
grid against $5.7$--$7.9$~g in Norway, an $18.8\times$ range the
privacy--utility framing cannot express.

UCI-HAR~\cite{uci-har} is our primary dataset: its clients are the individual
volunteers the data came from, so the client boundary is a real person rather
than a synthetic partition. CIFAR-10 carries the results needing a harder
task.

Measuring a privacy mechanism also exposes two hazards, and we report both
because any such study faces them. Deriving the noise multiplier costs more
for a loose budget than a tight one, so run inside the federated loop it lands
in the measurement window and takes the shape of a cost of privacy. And the
reported $\varepsilon$ is not the spent one: client sampling is binomial, so
the clients participating most overspend the budget the accountant was paid
for --- in all 29 of our private runs, by up to $22\%$.
